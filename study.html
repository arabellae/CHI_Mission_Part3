How the Study Worked

Three shapes, three scents, three feelings, and one story.

The Things the Children Worked With

Touch (Hidden in a Box)

3 Shapes

Smell (4 Seconds, Re-Sniff Allowed)

3 Scents

Feeling (as a “Village”)

3 Feelings

The shapes were 3D-printed, about the same weight, and sanded smooth.

The Story: Four Tasks

  1. Name the shapes. The child feels each shape and says whether it is “Bouba,” “Kiki,” neither, or gives it their own name.
  2. Shapes to feelings. Each shape “moves to” one of three villages: an excited village, a calm village, or a neither village.
  3. Scents to feelings. Each scent is a “potion” in the air. Which village does it belong to?
  4. Shapes to scents. After painting, which soap (scent) goes with which shape for a bath?

Setup Details

  • 26 children (16 girls, 10 boys), ages 2 to 4½, from four nurseries; all fluent in English.
  • A familiar room, with a staff member present. A camera was pointed so it did not show the child’s face.
  • The shapes were treated as characters. Feelings were places, not pets, to avoid nudging answers.
  • The order of tasks 2 to 4 and of the shapes was shuffled between children. The scents were always in the same order.

Each child gave several answers, so the study has 78 answers per comparison (26 children × 3). Those answers come from the same children and are not fully independent of each other. This is a reason for modest conclusions (see How Much to Trust It).
