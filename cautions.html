How Much to Trust It

Yes, a little. This is one small study.

What the Authors Say

  • Some children could not explain their choices.
  • Only a few shapes and scents were used.
  • Children had to answer by speaking, so children who do not speak were left out.
  • Children’s reasons often have several layers; the coding simplified them.
  • The effect of using a story was not formally tested.
  • They call for follow-up studies over time as children grow.

Other Things to Keep in Mind

  • Small, specific group. Twenty-six children from four nurseries, all fluent in English.
  • Tendencies, not rules. The most common answer for every shape was “neither.”
  • One link was not significant. Shape and feeling (p = .065) is a trend only.
  • Matches, not causes. The study does not show that lemon makes children excited.
  • Design ideas are suggestions. The authors say scent could help children share feelings. That was not tested.
  • Not fully independent answers. Each child gave three answers per test. This is a note from the guide’s author, not from the paper’s authors.
